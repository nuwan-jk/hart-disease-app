MEDICAL CLINICAL GUIDELINE - DOCUMENT 14
SUBJECT: Atrial Fibrillation - Pathophysiology, Diagnosis, and Management

1. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & SYMPTOMATOLOGY
Patients afflicted with Atrial Fibrillation typically manifest with the following clinical symptoms: palpitations, irregular pulse, shortness of breath, dizziness, syncope, fluttering in chest. 
These symptoms often present acutely and require immediate clinical assessment. A thorough history of the present illness should focus on the onset, provocation, quality, radiation, and severity of these symptoms.

2. DIAGNOSTIC MODALITIES
Gold standard diagnostics include: ECG showing irregularly irregular rhythm without P waves. 
Clinicians must evaluate laboratory markers and imaging concurrently to rule out differential diagnoses such as pulmonary embolism or acute aortic dissection.

3. EVIDENCE-BASED TREATMENT PROTOCOLS
Standard of care dictates the following pharmacological and surgical interventions: Anticoagulants, Rate control (Diltiazem, Metoprolol).
Patient prognosis improves by 40% when treatment is initiated within the first 24 hours of symptom onset. Long-term management requires continuous hemodynamic monitoring and lifestyle modifications including smoking cessation and dietary adjustments.
